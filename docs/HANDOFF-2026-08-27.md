## What broke, and how badly

`scripts/decode_depth_sweep.py` requested `max_tokens=256`, but its prompt ended
*"In one sentence, state what this describes."* The model obeyed. **All 70 reps** of the
2026-08-26 sweep returned 25-26 tokens. Nothing asserted the output length.

At MTP=5 a 25-token window is ~5 speculative cycles — it measures draft-acceptance
variance, not throughput.

| depth | old (25-tok) | corrected (256-tok) | overstated by |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,048 | 76.35 | **54.72** | 28.3% |
| 8,192 | 72.64 | **56.02** | 22.9% |
| 32,768 | 70.21 | **54.28** | 22.7% |
| 131,072 | 72.64 | **53.06** | 27.0% |
| 262,144 | 84.37 | **50.66** | **40.0%** |

Two consequences beyond the magnitudes:

1. **The corrected curve is flat** (50.7-56.0 across a 128x depth range). The old data's
   headline — decode *fastest* at 262K — was an artifact. Corrected, 262K is the lowest.
2. **The bias grows with depth** (22.7% to 40.0%), so conclusions resting on the curve's
   *shape* are affected, not only its scale. **`+33.6% at 131K` compared two
   differently-biased numbers and is withdrawn.**

The instrument is fixed at `c3e8e0d`: `min_tokens == max_tokens` + `ignore_eos`, and it
**raises** if `completion_tokens != max_tokens`. Verified against the live engine before
use (64 requested, 64 returned).

## Open problem 1 — the 2-node arm does not exist

**This is the blocker.** Corrected 3-node numbers exist; there is nothing valid to compare
them against. Both old arms carried the 25-token defect, and the old 2-Spark baseline
(`20260821T001024Z-2spark-baseline`) is worse still: **all 90 reps returned 10 output
tokens** (~2 MTP cycles).

Needs a cluster restart via `scripts/cluster_tp2.sh` (workers first — ordering is
load-bearing), a fabric gate with the engine **stopped** between arms, then the same
sweep. Until then **no 2-vs-3 claim is supportable in either direction.**

## Open problem 2 — intermittent decode collapse at 262K

Uncharacterized, found only because the window was fixed.

At 262K, **4 of 9 requests collapsed ~40x** (`decode_s` 218 s and 78 s against a 5 s
baseline) while **TTFT stayed normal at 165-181 s on every rep**. Prefill healthy, decode
stalled.

Ruled out: fabric (TTFT normal), prompt, cache (`cached_tokens: 0` throughout), wedged
engine (HTTP 200 across the run), JIT (warmup 2 was *slower* than warmup 1; recovery took
four requests). 131K shows the same signature one order milder — three reps at 6.7-7.9 s
`decode_s` against a 5.2 s baseline — visible only because #24 asked for 15 reps.

**A 26-token window finishes in ~0.5 s, before this regime appears.** The old harness
could not have seen it at any rep count. **Do not rely on 262K numbers until this is
characterized.**

## On the 2-node repository

[`colonel-otto/DeepSeek-v4-Flash-DSpark-2x-DGX-Spark`](https://github.com/colonel-otto/DeepSeek-v4-Flash-DSpark-2x-DGX-Spark).

**The rule, learned the hard way: never compare a raw number across the two repos.** I
claimed their numbers were 20-38% faster than ours; that was wrong — their runs generate
512 completion tokens, ours generated 25, and their figure sits inside our own spread.
Cross-repo material is valuable for **config and methodology**, never for numbers. To
compare, run *their* harness on *our* cluster and publish both arms.

**Where they are ahead of us:** they force output length with `min_tokens`+`ignore_eos`
and verify it — the exact guard whose absence caused our defect. Their `EVAL.md`
documents the ~4.5x SSE chunk-counting undercount and the prompt-sensitivity effect
(**five days before we found it**).

**Where doubt is warranted, with evidence:**

| claim | status |
|---|---|
| **315 / 205 tok/s** (their README headline) | no script, no prompt, no raw data |
| CUDA-graph A/B +28.6% | prose only — and it justifies a shipped default |
| 900K TTFT 1,028.85 s | in no committed file |
| "68-75% acceptance, verified 08-16" | no 08-16 artifact exists |
| 08-14 matrix | good raw data; **producing script absent** (first committed 08-17, 3 days later) |

**Every published cell in both their tables is n=1.** Their own script supports
`--repeat 5`; no committed result uses it.

**Their own data contains an unattributed window effect.** At 131K c=1: **74.9** on their
128-token matrix vs **65.2** on their 512-token sweep — same cluster, **+15% from window
length alone**, in the same direction as our own finding.

**Their published data is cold, n=1.** The 08-14 run: 20 cells in 25 minutes
(13:10:37 to 13:35:23) — no room for warm-up. Their own EVAL.md prescribes warming at
least 2 requests per shape; none of their published data does it. **This cuts against us**
if we warm and they do not.
